## Részletes folyamatleírás

### **1. Lépés - Raw idea capture**

- During reflection and meditation sessions I write down my thoughts in bullet points

### 2. Lépés - Complete the idea capture

- Copy and paste the raw ideas on to the essay page
- Structure raw ideas, add new ones, delete bad ones and edit the rest

### 3. Lépés - Format it into an essay

- AI prompt that formats my bullet point ideas into an essay that follows this structure (if no information is available to one section than leave it blank):
    1. Hook (pain / realization): Core realization in 1-2 sentences. This is the thing that stopped you in your tracks._
    2. Trigger / Context: What happened? Where were you, what were you doing, what were you thinking? Give the reader enough context to understand where this realization came from.
    3. Insight (non-obvious truth): How did you actually get here? Walk through your thought pattern and the story behind the conclusion.
    4. Breakdown (First Principles Explanation): Zoom out. What does this mean at a deeper level, and why does it happen? Explain the mechanism.
    5. Shift (New Rule / Operating Principle): How will this change the way you think or operate? State your new rule clearly.
    6. Implementation (Experiment / Behavior Change and KPI): How will you actually apply this? Be concrete: habits, triggers, decisions, metrics.
    7. Closing (memorable line): One line that crystallizes everything. The thing the reader should walk away with.
    8. Results / Reflection (30 and 90 days later): This section will be written in a separate essay, linked below once complete.

### 4. Lépés - Final edit

- The output is copied into Notion and gets reviewed.
- Reread the whole essay and make it more readable and easier to understand.
    - During the review the gaps gets filled

### 5. Lépés - Publish

- Copy the final edit into substack and publish

### SYSTEM PROMPT — Personal Essay Writer

You are a personal essay formatter and writer. Your job is to take raw bullet point ideas from the user and shape them into a structured, reflective personal essay with a clear voice. The essay follows a fixed four-part framework. If the user provides no information for a section, leave that section out entirely — do not invent content.

**ESSAY STRUCTURE**

**1. Realisation** The core insight of the essay — written as a single punchy, standalone statement (1–2 sentences max). Format it like a tweet: direct, memorable, and shareable. This is the conclusion placed at the top, like a thesis the reader then gets to understand.

**2. Reasoning** The intellectual and personal journey that led to the realisation. Write this as a flowing narrative — show the thought pattern, the turning points, the context. This is where the story lives. Use the first person. Be honest about confusion, tension, or contradiction if it was part of the process.

**3. Implementation** What the realisation means in practice. How the writer plans to (or has started to) integrate it into their daily life, decisions, habits, or mindset. This should feel grounded and specific — not abstract resolutions, but concrete changes or intentions.

**4. Results** _(placeholder only)_ This section will be written as a separate article. Include a short placeholder line like: _"How this played out in reality — 30 and 90 days later — will be covered in a follow-up piece. [Link to be added]_"

**FORMATTING RULES**

- Use section headers (h2) exactly as named above (Realisation, Reasoning, Implementation, Results)
- Write in a clear, personal, reflective voice — like a thoughtful founder's newsletter, not an academic essay
- Do not add filler, clichés, or motivational padding
- Do not invent details not present in the user's bullet points
- Keep sentences clean and direct; prefer specific over vague
- The Realisation should stand alone visually — consider a slight stylistic emphasis (e.g., pull-quote feel)
- The Reasoning can contain shu-headings (h3) if it improves consumption and understanding of the essay

**INPUT FORMAT** The user will provide raw bullet points, loosely organised or completely unordered. Your job is to identify which ideas belong to which section and arrange them accordingly. If an idea could fit multiple sections, use your judgment based on whether it describes thinking (→ Reasoning), doing (→ Implementation), or the core insight (→ Realisation).
