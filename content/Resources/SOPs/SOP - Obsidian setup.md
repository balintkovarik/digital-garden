## Setup — one-time

- [ ] **Create the 6 folders** — Rough Notes, Source Material, Tags, Indexes, Templates, and your main notes folder. The structure keeps your system from becoming an unnavigable mess as it grows.
- [ ] **Set default new note location to your main folder** — Settings → Files and Links → "in the folder specified below". Saves you from manually moving every new note.
- [ ] **Create a note template and bind it to a hotkey** — Having date, title, status, tags, and a references section pre-filled means zero friction when starting a new note. Ctrl+T should be automatic muscle memory.
- [ ] **Store your vault in Google Drive or OneDrive** — Automatic backup and cross-device access. Your entire knowledge base travels with you without any manual effort.

## Workflow — how you write notes

- [ ] **Always write source material notes before main notes** — Source notes (page number + quote + your expansion) are the raw input. Main notes are the distilled insight. Skipping source notes means losing context for where ideas came from.
- [ ] **Write main notes in your own words, one idea per note** — If you can't articulate it, you haven't understood it. One idea per note keeps notes reusable across projects — a note trying to capture five things is useful for none of them.
- [ ] **Keep notes under ~500 words, fit on one screen** — Long notes become mini-essays that you stop revisiting. Short notes stay flexible and easy to link into whatever you're writing.
- [ ] **Always fill the References section with links to source and related notes** — This is what makes the network valuable. Without references, notes are isolated — with them, rediscovering old ideas becomes accidental and delightful.

## Habits — ongoing

- [ ] **Use tags tied to your interests, not generic categories** — A tag like "breathing brand positioning" is more useful than "business". Your tags should reflect how you actually think, not how a library would organize things.
- [ ] **Turn high-traffic tags into indexes once they exceed ~10 notes** — An index acts like a book's table of contents for that topic. Without it, large clusters of notes become intimidating and hard to navigate — which defeats the whole system.
- [ ] **Cap tags at 4–5 per note** — More tags create noise and dilute the signal. If you can't pick 4 meaningful ones, the note probably needs to be split or rewritten more precisely.
- [ ] **Search for similar notes before creating a new one (Ctrl+O)** — Prevents writing the same note twice. Months from now you will forget you already captured an idea — the search step is your protection against that.
